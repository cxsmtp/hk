# Hawk AI version log

Every release, newest first: the version shown bottom-left in the console (HK-xx.xx.xx, from the VERSION file), when it was merged, and what changed for the people using it. Updated with every change merged to main (CLAUDE.md).

## HK-00.03.00 — pending · [#NN](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/NN)

- **Your sign-in is no longer kept where a script could read it.** The console keeps its sign-in in secure cookies that only the API can read (no longer in the browser's storage), every change you make carries a check that another site cannot forge, and Sign out now ends the sign-in on the server too. Everyone signs in once more after this update. The SDK, the MCP proxy, the endpoint agent and the browser extension work as before.
- **Evidence an auditor can check without trusting us.** The auditor's export (with a signed list of all its files), each file downloaded from it, and the audit log, activity and connector exports now carry a digital signature made with a private key only Hawk AI holds: by default a key made for this installation and kept in its backups, or a key in AWS KMS, Azure Key Vault or Google Cloud KMS. Anyone can check them offline with the public key alone (tools/verify_evidence.py; the keys are at /.well-known/hawk-evidence-keys.json). Settings → Audit log shows the key's fingerprint, and owners and admins can rotate it; evidence signed before stays checkable.
- **An administrator's guide inside the console.** Settings → Guide (also from Get help and Jump to) explains, one page per topic, how to install Hawk AI on one Linux machine, the first sign-in, people and sign-in, HTTPS, email, backups and restore, updates, the licence, the audit log and evidence, the network, the self-check and Get help, and what to do when something goes wrong. Everyone signed in can read it; what only owners and admins can do is marked. The same guide can be saved as one printable page.
- **Logs you can follow.** The API, worker and scheduler write one line per event as JSON (LOG_FORMAT, LOG_LEVEL): every request with its route, status, time taken and a request id that comes back in the X-Request-ID header and in error messages; every background task's start, end or failure; sign-ins, lockouts, licence, people and role changes, backups and restores, updates, software integrity, HTTPS, the self-check's repairs, gateway decisions and connector errors. Passwords, tokens, keys, cookies and prompt text never reach the log, and Docker keeps at most 100 MB of it per service.
- **Single sign-on works on the one-machine install.** `COMPANY_SSO_ENABLED`, the `OIDC_*` settings, `SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES` and `RESET_TWO_STEP` in `deploy/.env` now reach Hawk AI (they were ignored before); `deploy/.env.example` shows an example provider. The restore commands Hawk AI prints use the right folder (`/backups`), and "Download troubleshooting report" on Settings → Backups & health works. Pointers to documents that no longer ship now lead to the matching page of the guide.
- **Stronger checks for sensitive data and prompt attacks, all on your server.** Card numbers, IBANs, Emirates IDs, US social security and UK national insurance numbers, passports (including the machine-readable lines), phone numbers, emails, IP addresses, keys and passwords are now confirmed by their check digits, format rules and nearby words before they count, even when disguised with look-alike letters, invisible characters, spacing or base64. Prompts are also recognised as source code, financial statements, deal (M&A) documents, HR or medical records, legally privileged text or credentials, and each tenant can turn categories off, add its own word lists, and upload lists of its own sensitive values (customer IDs, say), which are kept only as salted fingerprints, never as the values (`/detection/settings`, `/detection/fingerprints`). Prompt-injection and jailbreak attempts are caught by a versioned rule set covering English and 12 other languages plus a small built-in classifier, each with a risk score and stable reason codes, and each tenant chooses the score that blocks, holds for approval, redacts or only logs. This applies in the LLM and MCP gateways, the browser extension and the desktop guard. Nothing is sent to an outside AI service; names, places and organisations are recognised only where a spaCy model is installed (the standard image has none, and `/health` and `/detection/coverage` say so).
- **The console is tested in a real browser.** On every change to the console or the API, and before anything is published, Chromium signs in to a fresh installation behind one address, as deployed: the first sign-in (new password, terms of use), a wrong password, staying signed in across a reload, Sign out, the CSRF check, every Settings page, adding a person, Roles & permissions, Branding, two-step setup, Activation codes, and what a Viewer is offered.
- **Streamed answers go through the LLM gateway too.** Chat apps and coding agents that ask for a streamed answer (`stream: true`) are no longer turned away: the request gets the same checks as before the model is called, the answer arrives piece by piece in the usual OpenAI format, and every piece is checked before it is sent, with the last part held back until the full answer is decided. If something sensitive appears, even split across pieces, the stream stops with a "content filter" ending and Hawk's reason, and the decision and evidence are recorded once, just as for an answer that is not streamed.
- **Attack simulations you can trust the count of.** Each of the 244 catalogued attack scenarios now either runs against Hawk AI's own controls on every change (182; before, 124 were labelled executable but none was ever run by a test) or says plainly why not: 49 run but show where Hawk AI behaves differently from what the catalogue expects, and 13 need something outside Hawk AI (a model registry, an image OCR engine). Simulations shows both, never as executable, and simulation_catalog/executability_report.json lists every scenario. Running them found and fixed three gaps: the browser extension now blocks the jailbreaks, system-prompt extraction and role spoofing the gateway already blocked, cloud keys and passwords pasted into a prompt are recognised as secrets, and an oversized prompt is blocked even when it does not come through the web API.
- **Status.** Not merged yet.

## HK-00.02.01 — 2026-11-26 09:47 UTC · [#9](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/9)

- **No known vulnerable component.** The console moves to Next.js 15.5.29 and sharp 0.35.5 (a critical remote-code-execution flaw and several image-library flaws fixed). The API's PDF reader, certificate bundle, DNS, ASN.1 and HTTP libraries move to their fixed versions, and sign-in tokens are now made and checked with PyJWT instead of python-jose, which has an unfixed advisory. Nothing changes for people signing in.
- **Ticketing only over https.** Jira and ServiceNow addresses must be https (or this machine); a file: or cloud metadata address is refused.
- **Every change is checked for security.** A new check on every pull request, on main and weekly: no secret in new commits, no known-vulnerable Python or JavaScript component that ships, and no medium or high finding in the API code.
- **Faster, cheaper checks.** Tests run once per change instead of twice, and the API tests run in parallel.
- **Status.** Merged with every API, console, image and security check passing (5,481 API tests; Checkmarx One counted 60 issues fixed, 6 of them critical).

## HK-00.02.00 — 2026-11-25 22:36 UTC · [#5](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/5), [#7](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/7), [#8](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/8)

- **Updates from the console.** Settings → Updates (owners and admins) lists every published version of Hawk AI, newest three first and ten more at a time, and installs a newer one or rolls back to an older one in one click.
- **An update never loses the version that ran.** The new updater service takes a backup, installs every image, brings the database up to date and starts each service in order; if Hawk AI is not healthy within 150 seconds, it puts the previous version back by itself, with the database as it was before the update.
- **A version that failed is never installed again by itself.** Auto-update is off until an owner or admin turns it on; it then looks a minute after start and every 15 minutes, follows the latest or the stable release, waits for an hour of the day if one is chosen, and says on the page what it found.
- **Only what was released is installed.** Every image carries a signed list of its files; Hawk AI checks its own files and that signature at start, every 6 hours and on Check now, and shows Genuine, Files changed, Not signed by the release workflow, Not confirmed yet or Development copy, with an alert for owners and admins when something is wrong.
- **Hawk AI looks after itself.** Every 5 minutes it checks its database, Redis, worker and scheduler, the email server, its Checkmarx One connections, its automatic runs and its error rate, puts right what it can, emails the owners and admins after two checks in a row, goes back a version that broke what worked (never for an outage elsewhere), and can raise a support case for them to forward; Settings → Backups & health shows it.
- **Hands-off.** Settings → Hands-off sends a short status every week, with buttons that ask "Yes, do it" before they act and one-word replies (PAUSE, RESUME, RUN, STATUS, STOP, SOLVED), so nobody has to sign in.
- **Releases are built and signed by GitHub.** A release workflow publishes the api, web, proxy and updater images for amd64 and arm64 with a build attestation; a stable release is promoted by hand, without rebuilding.
- **People, Teams and Roles & permissions, as in FTR Command Center.** Settings → People lists everyone in the teams you work in: add a person with a temporary password (and a welcome email), change their name, sign-in email (both addresses are told), role, team and manager, reset their password or two-step sign-in, unlock them, sign them out, turn access off or remove them, one at a time or many at once. Settings → Teams builds the team tree with its leaders; a leader manages the people of their team and the teams below it. Settings → Roles & permissions shows every role against every permission: tick, find a permission, show only the differences, make a new role from another, rename. Nobody gives more than they hold, only an Owner gives the Owner role, and there is always an active Owner. The five built-in roles hold exactly what they could do before: nobody gains or loses anything.
- **People join by themselves; whoever manages people reviews them.** Off until an Owner or Admin turns it on (Settings → Sign-in): the email domains and the roles open to joining. People who join count against the activation code's users and set up two-step sign-in first when it is required; People lists them at the top to approve or turn off.
- **Settings in one place, as in FTR Command Center.** "Settings" in the main menu opens its own menu beside the page — People, Licensing, Records and System — showing only what your role allows, and Jump to (Ctrl K) finds any page. New: General (platform mode, server address), Branding (name, logo up to 200 KB, browser icon up to 100 KB, look; shown in the menu, the browser tab and every email), Email (SMTP) (saved as you type and tested at once, the last settings that worked kept, the password encrypted; one email server for sign-in, people, support, hands-off and backups, falling back to SMTP_*) and About (version, licence, terms, what is new). The self-check now also looks at every connector that can sign in and read, and support emails go to everyone whose role holds "Answer support cases".
- **Status.** Merged with every API, console and image check passing; published and signed as HK-00.02.00. A fresh install, every Settings page, backup with restore, and update with rollback were checked by hand.
