Every release, newest first: the version shown bottom-left in the console (HK-xx.xx.xx, from the VERSION file), when it was merged, and what changed for the people using it. Updated with every change merged to main (CLAUDE.md).

## HK-00.03.04 — 2026-11-27 16:40 UTC · [#13](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/13)

- **Settings that apply to the whole server are changed only by the server's administrators.** The password rule, idle sign-out, the sign-in notice and single sign-on, the compliance settings (how long activity is kept, the data location, the incident contact and the recommended baseline), the email server, branding, HTTPS, the network and proxy, updates, backups and restore, hands-off, activation codes and the evidence signing key can now be changed only by the Owners and Admins of the first workspace on the server. Owners and Admins of any other workspace still see these pages, read-only and marked "Set by the server's administrators", without the save buttons; a change they try anyway is refused and recorded in the audit log. Each workspace's own settings (its people, teams and roles, AI system approvals, detection settings and compliance records) stay with its own admins, and on a server with one workspace nothing changes.
- **Card-number masking no longer mistakes other numbers for cards.** A number is now masked as a card only when it is written the way cards are (together, or in the 4-4-4-4, 4-4-4-4-3, Amex 4-6-5 or Diners 4-6-4 groups), starts with a real card network's prefix at a length that network issues, and passes the card checksum. IBANs, dates and timestamps, long record ids, phone numbers and numbers labelled as an order, invoice, reference or account number are left as they are, while a number labelled as a card is always masked. On test sets of each kind, wrongly masked numbers fell from up to 12% to under 0.5%, and every real card number in every layout is still masked.

Anyone can switch to dark, or to their device's setting, under Appearance in the account menu, and the browser remembers it; an Owner or Admin sets everyone's default on Settings → Branding.

Single sign-on buttons appear on their own when there is one workspace; with several, you type your work email and press Continue with single sign-on, and your organisation's provider is found from the email's domain (the answer never reveals whether an address has an account). Someone who belongs to several workspaces gets the one they used last and can switch from the account menu.

## HK-00.03.02 — 2026-11-27 15:30 UTC · [#12](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/12)

- **A compliance pack for each framework you work to, with signed evidence.** Settings → Compliance now opens on framework packs for India's DPDP Act and Rules, the UAE PDPL (with notes on the DIFC and ADGM laws), Dubai's DESC ISR, ISO/IEC 27001 and 42001, PCI DSS 4.0.1, the CIS Controls 8.1, the NIST AI RMF with its Generative AI Profile, NIST CSF 2.0, Saudi Arabia's NCA ECC and SAMA framework, Australia's Essential Eight, CPS 234 and voluntary AI safety guardrails, the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications, MITRE ATLAS and, optionally, the EU AI Act. Each lists the framework's requirements by their own reference, whether Hawk AI, you with Hawk AI, or your organisation does each one, the Hawk AI controls behind it and their status now, worked out from your settings and records, with a link to the setting that closes a gap; owners and admins record the organisation's own processes as in place or not applicable, reviewed yearly and kept in the audit log. Download signed evidence gives one zip per pack for a time window (requirement statuses, settings in force, audit entries, approvals, licence acceptances, your records), signed with the installation's evidence key and checkable offline with `tools/verify_evidence.py`; passwords, secrets and prompt text are never in it. The packs support your compliance work; they do not by themselves make you compliant or certified.
- **Hawk AI never keeps a full payment card number.** Card numbers that people paste into prompts, that AI answers contain, or that sit in files and browser or device telemetry are still seen by the detectors, which warn or block as before, but nothing Hawk AI keeps shows more than the last four digits (`[card ending 1111]`): every database record, the encrypted copy of a gateway prompt, every log line and the error log on disk, the audit and activity exports, the auditor's export, SIEM exports, the diagnostics bundle, system emails and the background OCR job (which now travels encrypted). A card number written in groups, inside JSON, split across a streamed answer or base64-encoded is caught too; record ids and fingerprints are left as they are. The hashes Hawk AI keeps of what it saw are still taken from the original text, and the audit log's chain, receipts and signed evidence still check. This supports PCI DSS v4.0.1 requirements 3.3, 3.4, 3.5 and 10 for the data Hawk AI handles; the guide's "Card numbers" section says what stays yours.
- **Fixes from the code scan.** Hiding card numbers can no longer be made to hang the server with a long run of short number groups, no longer changes phone numbers written with `+` or long decimals, and also covers text saved by the quick database updates (such as the reason an AI approval was revoked). A framework pack's evidence no longer includes other organisations' people's licence acceptances from before HK-00.03.01. The browser extension can no longer pick which policy rule applies to a signed-in person by naming a role or department, AI guidance for a finding or AI system is routed with at least the data sensitivity recorded for it, and the emailed copy of the licence agreement is sent once even when two parts of Hawk AI try at the same moment.

## HK-00.03.01 — 2026-11-27 06:36 UTC · [#11](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/11)

- **No AI system is approved by one person alone any more, unless you allow it.** Setting an AI system to approved now goes through an approval policy you set for each risk tier on Settings → AI system approvals: how many different people must approve (with two or more, the person who asked is never one of them), which roles may approve, and whether the approval is blanket (until revoked), one-time (used up by the first request through the gateway) or time-bound (ends on its own). By default higher tiers are stricter: low needs one other person and may be blanket; high, critical and restricted need two, and critical is time-bound only. A direct change of status to approved is refused; an approval that is used up, runs out (checked every minute) or is revoked puts the system back to pending re-approval and the gateway stops letting it through; every request, approval, denial, expiry and revocation is in the audit log. Systems approved before this version stay approved, as blanket approvals you can see and revoke there.
- **Hawk AI fixes what it recognises and tells you when it needs the OEM.** Every 5 minutes it reads its own error log and its failed background jobs and puts known problems right, each within a limit: it runs a failed job again, clears a stale lock, reconnects to the database or Redis, restarts a stuck worker or scheduler, frees disk space, checks the database schema again, and goes back to the previous version when an update fails or the new version is unhealthy, then checks it is healthy; the installer tries one fix of its own when the API does not come up. When a fix keeps failing or the problem is one it does not know, it stops, marks it "Needs OEM help" and prepares a diagnostics bundle: one file on Settings → Backups & health with recent errors, failed jobs, update and rollback history, health and the settings in force, every secret taken out, signed so the OEM can check it came from your server. Admins of other organisations on the same server now see a plain line with a time and a reference instead of the server's raw job errors, and get a bundle of their own organisation only.
- **A licence agreement every administrator accepts, with a copy by email.** Hawk AI now comes with a full End User Licence Agreement (the licence and its limits, the features, the limits of what AI detections can promise, a limited warranty, a liability cap of the fees paid in the last 12 months, UAE law and DIAC arbitration in Dubai) and terms of use for everyone else. At their first sign-in, and whenever the agreement changes, each owner or admin reads it to the end and accepts it with the company's name, their own name and email, confirming they are authorised; other people accept the shorter terms of use. Each acceptance is kept for good (the exact text and its SHA-256, who, when, from where), cannot be changed or deleted, and is in the audit log and the auditor's export. A copy goes to the administrator's email, now or as soon as the email server is set up and tested, with retries; Settings → About shows where it is and can send it again or download it. The licensor's name, address, notice email, governing law, forum and fixed cap are set once in `deploy/.env`.
- **The installation itself meets the CIS benchmarks, and every release says what is in it.** Every service runs as an ordinary user with no special rights, a read-only file system, memory and process limits and a health check; PostgreSQL and Redis sit on an internal network of their own, PostgreSQL accepts only strong passwords over an encrypted connection and logs who connects, and Redis refuses the commands that could wipe it. The updater, the one service that can manage the others, no longer runs as root and has no network at all. Base images move to current, patched versions (the console now runs on Node.js 22), every published image comes with a signed list of its components (CycloneDX SBOM), as do the API's and the console's dependencies, and nothing is published with a known, fixable high or critical vulnerability. The installer also checks the machine (firewall, SSH, automatic updates, time, disk encryption, audit daemon) and Settings → Backups & health shows the result; it changes nothing. Run `sudo deploy/install.sh` once to apply this. If `deploy/.env` names a published version older than this one, the installer leaves the updater off and updates go through the installer.
- **Two fixes from the code scan.** Only the people who may approve an AI system's current risk tier, or owners and admins, can lower that tier, so a system can no longer be moved under a laxer approval policy to get it approved. An owner or admin of another organisation on the same server now sees, downloads and re-sends only their own organisation's licence acceptances; the server's own administrators still see all of them.
- **Ready for Dubai (DESC) and India (CERT-In) audits.** A new Settings → Compliance page shows, control by control, where this installation stands against Dubai's Information Security Regulation, the UAE IA Standard and India's CERT-In directions, with a link to where each is set, and applies a recommended sign-in baseline in one click. Owners and admins can now set the password rule (length, kinds of character, no reuse of the last passwords, expiry after so many days), sign people out after a few minutes without activity, and show a notice on the sign-in page. Activity records are kept at least 180 days, the health check and the page say whether the server's clock is synchronised, About states where the data is, the audit log exports in CEF for a SIEM, and one button downloads a signed incident report for a time window (for CERT-In's 6-hour deadline or a report to DESC).
- **Status.** Merged with every API, console, browser, PostgreSQL, image and security check passing (6,083 API tests).

## HK-00.03.00 — 2026-11-26 18:52 UTC · [#10](https://github.com/cxsmtp/hk/pull/10)

- **Your sign-in is no longer kept where a script could read it.** The console keeps its sign-in in secure cookies that only the API can read (no longer in the browser's storage), every change you make carries a check that another site cannot forge, and Sign out now ends the sign-in on the server too. Everyone signs in once more after this update. The SDK, the MCP proxy, the endpoint agent and the browser extension work as before.
- **Evidence an auditor can check without trusting us.** The auditor's export (with a signed list of all its files), each file downloaded from it, and the audit log, activity and connector exports now carry a digital signature made with a private key only Hawk AI holds: by default a key made for this installation and kept in its backups, or a key in AWS KMS, Azure Key Vault or Google Cloud KMS. Anyone can check them offline with the public key alone (tools/verify_evidence.py; the keys are at /.well-known/hawk-evidence-keys.json). Settings → Audit log shows the key's fingerprint, and owners and admins can rotate it; evidence signed before stays checkable.
- **An administrator's guide inside the console.** Settings → Guide (also from Get help and Jump to) explains, one page per topic, how to install Hawk AI on one Linux machine, the first sign-in, people and sign-in, HTTPS, email, backups and restore, updates, the licence, the audit log and evidence, the network, the self-check and Get help, and what to do when something goes wrong. Everyone signed in can read it; what only owners and admins can do is marked. The same guide can be saved as one printable page.
- **Logs you can follow.** The API, worker and scheduler write one line per event as JSON (LOG_FORMAT, LOG_LEVEL): every request with its route, status, time taken and a request id that comes back in the X-Request-ID header and in error messages; every background task's start, end or failure; sign-ins, lockouts, licence, people and role changes, backups and restores, updates, software integrity, HTTPS, the self-check's repairs, gateway decisions and connector errors. Passwords, tokens, keys, cookies and prompt text never reach the log, and Docker keeps at most 100 MB of it per service.
- **Single sign-on works on the one-machine install.** `COMPANY_SSO_ENABLED`, the `OIDC_*` settings, `SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES` and `RESET_TWO_STEP` in `deploy/.env` now reach Hawk AI (they were ignored before); `deploy/.env.example` shows an example provider. The restore commands Hawk AI prints use the right folder (`/backups`), and "Download troubleshooting report" on Settings → Backups & health works. Pointers to documents that no longer ship now lead to the matching page of the guide.
- **Stronger checks for sensitive data and prompt attacks, all on your server.** Card numbers, IBANs, Emirates IDs, US social security and UK national insurance numbers, passports (including the machine-readable lines), phone numbers, emails, IP addresses, keys and passwords are now confirmed by their check digits, format rules and nearby words before they count, even when disguised with look-alike letters, invisible characters, spacing or base64. Prompts are also recognised as source code, financial statements, deal (M&A) documents, HR or medical records, legally privileged text or credentials, and each tenant can turn categories off, add its own word lists, and upload lists of its own sensitive values (customer IDs, say), which are kept only as salted fingerprints, never as the values (`/detection/settings`, `/detection/fingerprints`). Prompt-injection and jailbreak attempts are caught by a versioned rule set covering English and 12 other languages plus a small built-in classifier, each with a risk score and stable reason codes, and each tenant chooses the score that blocks, holds for approval, redacts or only logs. This applies in the LLM and MCP gateways, the browser extension and the desktop guard. Nothing is sent to an outside AI service; names, places and organisations are recognised only where a spaCy model is installed (the standard image has none, and `/health` and `/detection/coverage` say so).
- **The console is tested in a real browser.** On every change to the console or the API, and before anything is published, Chromium signs in to a fresh installation behind one address, as deployed: the first sign-in (new password, terms of use), a wrong password, staying signed in across a reload, Sign out, the CSRF check, every Settings page, adding a person, Roles & permissions, Branding, two-step setup, Activation codes, and what a Viewer is offered.
- **Streamed answers go through the LLM gateway too.** Chat apps and coding agents that ask for a streamed answer (`stream: true`) are no longer turned away: the request gets the same checks as before the model is called, the answer arrives piece by piece in the usual OpenAI format, and every piece is checked before it is sent, with the last part held back until the full answer is decided. If something sensitive appears, even split across pieces, the stream stops with a "content filter" ending and Hawk's reason, and the decision and evidence are recorded once, just as for an answer that is not streamed.
- **Attack simulations you can trust the count of.** Each of the 244 catalogued attack scenarios now either runs against Hawk AI's own controls on every change (182; before, 124 were labelled executable but none was ever run by a test) or says plainly why not: 49 run but show where Hawk AI behaves differently from what the catalogue expects, and 13 need something outside Hawk AI (a model registry, an image OCR engine). Simulations shows both, never as executable, and simulation_catalog/executability_report.json lists every scenario. Running them found and fixed three gaps: the browser extension now blocks the jailbreaks, system-prompt extraction and role spoofing the gateway already blocked, cloud keys and passwords pasted into a prompt are recognised as secrets, and an oversized prompt is blocked even when it does not come through the web API.
- **AI agents change GitHub repositories only as you allow.** An agent cannot touch a repository or organization Hawk AI does not know; it never forks, deletes, transfers, changes visibility or settings of a repository, never pushes straight to the default or a protected branch (it opens a pull request instead), never changes credential files, and a change that carries a secret is stopped before it is pushed. Changes to CI workflows, CODEOWNERS or infrastructure code, and very large changes, wait for an approver; an approval covers exactly the change that was approved, once, for a limited time. Each repository can be read-only for agents, approval for every change (the default) or pull requests without approval, and each agent is limited in changes per minute. Every decision is kept as evidence, and Hawk AI's own GitHub connection now refuses a GitHub token that could write.
- **Security fixes from the code scan.** Requests that arrive at the same moment can no longer get around a limit: a refresh token gives one new sign-in only, wrong passwords and two-step codes are all counted (so the lock after five still holds) and a recovery code works once, the licence's caps on users and tenants and the "at least one Owner" rule hold, and two answers to one support request are both kept. The public health check and the health and auditor pages no longer show the database's or the server's own error messages (the request id points to them in the log), a log line in text format cannot be split by what someone typed, and neither the update check (even when redirected) nor the hands-off mailbox can be pointed at a cloud metadata address.
- **The server reads only its own simulation files.** A file path sent with a browser, gateway or agent request, or written into an attack simulation's upload step, is read only when it is a file in the simulation evidence folder; any other file on the server is never opened, scanned or uploaded. A malformed date in the activity or connector logs is now ignored instead of failing the page.
- **Status.** Merged with every API, console, browser, image and security check passing (5,989 API tests).

- **Hands-off.** Settings → Hands-off sends a short status every week, with buttons that ask "Yes, do it" before they act and one-word replies (PAUSE, RESUME, RUN, STATUS, STOP, SOLVED), so nobody has to sign in.

People who join count against the activation code's users and set up two-step sign-in first when it is required; People lists them at the top to approve or turn off.
- **Settings in one place, as in FTR Command Center.** "Settings" in the main menu opens its own menu beside the page — People, Licensing, Records and System — showing only what your role allows, and Jump to (Ctrl K) finds any page. New: General (platform mode, server address), Branding (name, logo up to 200 KB, browser icon up to 100 KB, look; shown in the menu, the browser tab and every email), Email (SMTP) (saved as you type and tested at once, the last settings that worked kept, the password encrypted; one email server for sign-in, people, support, hands-off and backups, falling back to SMTP_*) and About (version, licence, terms, what is new). The self-check now also looks at every connector that can sign in and read, and support emails go to everyone whose role holds "Answer support cases".
